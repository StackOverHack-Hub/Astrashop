# Student interview script

Speak with five to eight students at the campus the team can pilot. Include students from courses with specific supply requirements and students who usually obtain supplies without trouble. Allow about ten minutes each. Ask permission before writing down a direct quote, use a participant alias, and keep names and contact details out of the evidence log.

## Opening

“We’re looking at how students get supplies for campus courses. Could you walk me through the last time you needed a specific item for a class, lab, or submission?”

## Questions

1. What exact item, size, or model did you need? When did you first need it, and what was the latest time you could pick it up?
2. Where did you look first? What happened next? Walk me through each call, message, visit, or delivery search in order.
3. When did you first get a reliable answer that the exact item would be available? Who confirmed it, and what made the answer reliable?
4. Did you get the item before the deadline? What did the search cost in time, travel, or money? Which parts are estimates?
5. Has this happened with another item recently? Tell me about a case where your usual method worked well, too.
6. If you had to do it again tomorrow, what would you do first? What would make you change that approach?

If the student is willing, observe how they would check one current requirement and note the steps without collecting private message content. Ask the questions above before showing Astrashop. Afterward, show the reserve-and-pickup flow and ask the student where they would still call, visit, or wait for confirmation.

## Decision after interviews

Log each distinct recent incident with the item, deadline, workaround, and outcome. Proceed with the course-supply workflow if at least three recent incidents show real friction and one shopkeeper is willing to set items aside. If that evidence points to a different recurring task, record the change in the evidence log before shipping the pilot.
